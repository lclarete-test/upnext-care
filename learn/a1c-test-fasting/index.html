Do You Need to Fast Before an A1C Test?

An A1C test alone does not require fasting. Learn why other tests at the same appointment may change your preparation and what to ask the lab.

UpNext editorial · About 2 min read · October 20, 2026

EnglishPortuguêsEspañol

The short answer: no, for A1C alone

You do not need to fast for an A1C blood test by itself. You may also see it called HbA1c or hemoglobin A1C. It reflects average blood glucose over roughly the previous three months, rather than only the moment the sample is taken.1

The important distinction is between the test and the appointment. A visit can include several tests with different preparation instructions. Before changing breakfast plans, check the complete order.

Why the lab might still ask you to fast

A fasting glucose test is different from A1C. If it is ordered at the same visit, fasting may be required even though A1C itself does not need it. Other blood tests can also have specific instructions.1

Ask: Which tests are being collected? Do any require fasting? For how long? May I drink plain water? Follow the instructions for your actual appointment, rather than a general rule found online.

If you are told to fast

For blood tests that require fasting, the instructions usually allow plain water but exclude food and other drinks. The required duration depends on the test. Ask the clinic or laboratory to clarify anything uncertain before the appointment.2

Do not stop or change medicines on your own. Ask how to handle your usual medicines and supplements, especially if you use treatment that can lower blood glucose. Tell the lab if you ate or drank something outside its instructions; it can advise whether the appointment should change.2

Preparation is only one part of the result

Some conditions affecting red blood cells, including certain anemias, can make A1C less reliable. Tell your healthcare team about relevant health conditions and medicines. They may interpret the result alongside another test.3

Your follow-up questions matter too: What was this test checking? Does the result need confirmation? When should we discuss it? A number without a follow-up plan can leave more uncertainty than it resolves.

A useful next step

Check the order or call the lab before your visit. A simple message works: “I am having an A1C test. Are any other tests ordered, and what preparation do I need?” You do not need to guess or fast unnecessarily.
