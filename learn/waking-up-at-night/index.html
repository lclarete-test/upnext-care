Why Do I Keep Waking Up at Night?

Nighttime waking can have several causes. Learn what to track, which habits may help and when to ask about insomnia treatment.

UpNext editorial · About 2 min read · October 23, 2026

EnglishPortuguêsEspañol

A wake-up is a clue, not a diagnosis

Waking in the middle of the night does not point to one specific illness. What matters is the pattern: how often it happens, how long you stay awake and how you function the next day. A useful question is “What has changed?” rather than “What is wrong with me?”

Start with a simple sleep diary

For one to two weeks, note bedtime, awakenings, wake-up time and daytime sleepiness. Add naps, caffeine, alcohol and medicines. Bring the record to a clinician if sleep is affecting daily life. You do not need to diagnose the cause before asking for help.2

Make one manageable change

Try a consistent wake-up time and a quiet, dark room. Alcohol may help you drift off but can interrupt sleep later. Choose one adjustment you can sustain, then review the diary instead of judging progress from a single night.1

When bedtime tips are not enough

Persistent insomnia deserves treatment. Ask about cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia, or CBT-I, a structured first treatment for long-term insomnia. It goes beyond general sleep advice. Discuss medicines and supplements with your care team rather than adding several sleep products yourself.1

Take daytime safety seriously

If poor sleep makes driving or operating equipment unsafe, stop that activity and seek help. Sleep problems can affect attention and reactions. At your appointment, describe the impact: “I wake repeatedly and struggle to stay alert at work.”3
