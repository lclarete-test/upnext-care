Prediabetes: a chance to act early

What prediabetes means and how to turn a test result into a practical next step.

UpNext editorial · About 3 min read · October 10, 2026

Higher than usual, below the diabetes range

Prediabetes means your blood glucose is above the usual range but has not reached the range used to diagnose diabetes. It increases the chance of developing type 2 diabetes. It also deserves attention because it can travel alongside other risks to heart health.

The name can sound as though diabetes is already decided. It is not. Some people develop type 2 diabetes, others remain in the prediabetes range, and some return to lower glucose levels. Knowing about it gives you an opportunity to make a plan and follow up.

Feeling well does not rule it out

Prediabetes usually has no clear symptoms. You may feel fine and discover it during a routine blood test. That does not make the result meaningless. It means testing can reveal something that everyday feelings cannot.

A healthcare professional may use an A1C test, a fasting blood glucose test or another glucose test. Ask which test you had, what the result means, and when it should be checked again. Different tests provide different views of how your body handles glucose.

Small changes can have real value

Research on diabetes prevention found that a structured lifestyle program reduced the risk of type 2 diabetes in adults at high risk. The program combined changes to eating, physical activity and weight, with ongoing support. Its results do not promise the same outcome for every individual, but they show that prevention is more than wishful thinking.

For people with overweight, common prevention goals include losing about 5% to 7% of starting weight and building toward at least 150 minutes of moderate activity each week. These are goals to discuss and work toward gradually, not requirements you must meet immediately.

Your plan should fit your life

Begin by looking at what is realistic. Could you walk for ten minutes on three days this week? Would planning one easy lunch make afternoons simpler? Is there a drink you could change without feeling deprived? Pick an action that you can identify clearly and repeat.

Ask your clinician about other parts of the plan, including blood pressure, cholesterol and whether medication would be helpful in your circumstances. Lifestyle changes and medical care can work together. There is no need to treat them as competing choices.
