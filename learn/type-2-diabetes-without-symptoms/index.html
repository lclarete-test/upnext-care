Can You Have Type 2 Diabetes Without Symptoms?

Yes. Type 2 diabetes can develop without noticeable symptoms. Learn why feeling well does not replace testing and how to discuss screening with your care team.

UpNext editorial · About 2 min read · October 20, 2026

EnglishPortuguêsEspañol

Feeling well does not rule it out

Yes, you can have type 2 diabetes without noticing symptoms. It often develops slowly, and changes may be mild or absent for years. Waiting until you feel unwell can therefore delay finding it.1

That does not mean every ordinary tired afternoon is a warning sign. It means how you feel and what a blood test measures are different kinds of information. Neither worry nor reassurance is a diagnosis.

What symptoms can look like

Possible signs include increased thirst, urinating more often, tiredness, blurred vision and sores that heal slowly. These symptoms also have other possible explanations. Discuss new or persistent changes with a healthcare professional rather than using a checklist to diagnose yourself.1

Why screening can matter before symptoms

A clinician may recommend testing based on risk even when you feel well. Family history, previous gestational diabetes, prediabetes and other health factors can inform that conversation. The right timing depends on your circumstances; you do not have to wait for a dramatic symptom.2

Bring any previous results and ask whether you are due for screening. If you were told to repeat a test, check when and why. A past result describes that point in your history, not a permanent guarantee.

Tests answer a different question

Healthcare professionals use blood tests such as A1C or fasting plasma glucose to investigate diabetes. The choice depends on your situation, and a second test is usually needed to confirm a diagnosis. A home glucose meter cannot establish the diagnosis.2

Ask what the result means and what happens next. If diabetes is diagnosed, care can include practical routines, monitoring and medicines, with support from your healthcare team. Needing treatment is not a personal failure.3

When not to wait for routine screening

Trouble breathing, vomiting, abdominal pain or fruity-smelling breath can occur with diabetic ketoacidosis, an emergency that can also affect people with type 2 diabetes. Seek urgent medical help for these concerning symptoms rather than waiting for a routine blood test.1

Turn uncertainty into a question

You can start with: “I feel well, but does my history suggest I should be tested?” You do not need to arrive with an answer. The goal is an appropriate next step, rather than constant checking or waiting for symptoms to prove something.
