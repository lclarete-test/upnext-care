Hair Loss: Could a Vitamin or Mineral Deficiency Be Involved?

Deficiencies can contribute to hair loss, but supplements are not a universal fix. Learn what patterns to report and how to discuss testing.

UpNext editorial · About 2 min read · October 23, 2026

EnglishPortuguêsEspañol

There is more than one possible cause

Inadequate iron, zinc or protein can contribute to hair loss. So can thyroid disease, hereditary patterns and other conditions. More hair in the shower does not identify which explanation applies to you.1

Look back as well as at today

Increased shedding may appear months after illness, childbirth, major stress or substantial weight loss. The delay can make the connection easy to miss. Describe the timeline rather than assuming the last shampoo you used is responsible.3

Testing should answer a question

A dermatologist can examine the scalp and decide whether blood tests or other investigations are needed. Supplements may help when a deficiency is confirmed; taking them without a need is not a general treatment for hair loss. Ask what each proposed test would clarify.2

Care for the hair you have

Use gentle handling and avoid styles that pull tightly. Harsh treatments and excessive heat can add damage. Eating too little can also undermine hair health. These steps support care, but they cannot replace treatment of an underlying condition.4

Prepare a useful appointment note

Record when shedding began, whether it is diffuse or patchy and whether the scalp hurts or itches. List medicines, supplements and recent diet changes. Seek assessment for sudden, patchy or persistent loss instead of cycling through products. Ask: “What is the likely cause, and how will we judge whether the plan is working?”
